# EP001 — "The Four Doors"

**GROUNDTRUTH · Pillar 1: CHOKEPOINTS · Framework A1 + B4 · Runtime 0:80 · 9:16**

---

## Why this idea

It cannot be told any other way.

Four separate theatres, thousands of kilometres apart, each with a measured chokepoint; a
reversal that is a **5,000 km camera move** connecting two places nobody would put in the same
sentence; and a conclusion that is literally a spatial relationship. A host cannot say this. A
bar chart cannot show it. The map *is* the argument — which is the entire editorial test in
Part 1.1 of the handbook.

It is also durable: this is not news. The geography was true in 1850 and will be true in 2100,
so the video does not decay, and it does not require us to take a side on anything current.

## Thumbnail concept

*(Shorts still-frame; also the cover for the YouTube feed card.)*

Pulled from **Scene 12**, not rendered separately — the thumbnail must pay off inside the video.

- Northern hemisphere, polar-ish framing, `VOID` background
- Russia in `LAND`, unlit
- **Four `SIGNAL` reticles** — Danish Straits, Bosphorus, Tsushima, GIUK gap — each a closed
  bracket pair, all four visible at once
- No arrows, no lines connecting them. The pattern does the work.
- Type, top band: **FOUR DOORS** — Archivo Expanded, 128px, `PAPER`
- No face, no red arrow, no open mouth. The restraint *is* the differentiator in a Shorts feed.

## Hook — first 5 seconds

| t | Audio | Visual |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | *(silence)* | Frame opens **already moving** — camera descending over Siberia, `CLEAR` parting |
| 0.4 | Music: sub-bass enters | Russia's border completes its `TRACE` |
| 0.8 | **"This is the largest country on Earth."** | Border snaps to `SIGNAL`; `17,098,246 km²` counts up in the data band |
| 2.6 | **"Thirty-seven thousand kilometres of coastline."** | Coastline strobes `COLD` along its full length, 900ms sweep |
| 4.2 | *(music cuts to silence)* | Hard hold, camera stops |
| 4.4 | **"Almost none of it opens onto the ocean."** | Coastline `COLD` → `LAND` over 300ms. The map goes dead. |

**Why it holds:** the claim contradicts itself inside four seconds. Biggest country + most
coastline + no ocean access is a logical impossibility, and the viewer stays to find out which
part is false. The music cut at 4.2 is the single most important frame in the video.

## Full narration script

**166 words · ~63s of speech · ~80s with holds**

> This is the largest country on Earth. Thirty-seven thousand kilometres of coastline.
>
> Almost none of it opens onto the ocean.
>
> Russia's navy is split into four fleets. Each one has exactly one way out.
>
> The Baltic Fleet has to pass here. Four kilometres wide. Every shore belongs to NATO.
>
> The Black Sea Fleet has one exit. The Bosphorus is seven hundred metres across, and it runs
> through the middle of Istanbul.
>
> Who may pass, and when, was decided by a treaty signed in 1936.
>
> The Pacific Fleet is here, at Vladivostok. Every route to open water runs past Japan.
>
> And in winter, the harbour needs icebreakers.
>
> Which leaves the north. This is Murmansk — two hundred and seventy kilometres inside the
> Arctic Circle.
>
> It never freezes. Because the water arriving here left the Gulf of Mexico.
>
> One warm current gives Russia its only year-round ocean port.
>
> To reach the Atlantic, its ships pass between Greenland, Iceland and Britain.
>
> Four doors. Russia owns none of them.

### Subtitles

- Burned in, always. 44px Inter Tight Medium, `PAPER`, `VOID` at 70% behind.
- Bottom of the data band (y ≈ 1620) — **never** below 1700.
- Max 2 lines, max 7 words per line.
- Break on grammatical clauses, never mid-phrase.
- Subtitle text = narration verbatim. On-screen *graphic* text is separate and must not duplicate.

### Grade

- Single node. Lift the blacks to `#0A0E13`, not to 0 — pure black crushes on OLED phones.
- No film grain, no vignette, no bloom.
- Saturation −4 globally, then `SIGNAL` qualified and pushed +12. The accent must be the only
  saturated thing in frame.

## Why this video works

### Psychological hook
The first four seconds state a **logical impossibility**: the largest country on Earth, 37,000
km of coast, no ocean access. Three facts that cannot all be true. The brain will not scroll
past an unresolved contradiction — it needs to know which one is false. (The answer: none of
them. That's the payoff.)

### Pacing
Thirteen scenes in 84 seconds — a beat every **6.5s**, with an information change every
**1.4s** on average. The four "doors" give a rhythm the viewer learns by the second one, so from
S5 onward they are *anticipating* rather than following. That anticipation is retention.

### Curiosity gap
Opened at 0:04 and held for **53 seconds**. That is unusually long for the format, and it works
because each door is a partial payoff — the viewer gets satisfaction on a schedule while the
main question stays open. The gap closes at 0:57, at 68% of runtime: exactly where the handbook
puts the reversal.

### Emotional arc
Grandeur (largest country) → confinement (four doors, each narrower) → the film gets
progressively **tighter and colder** through S8 → then the widest shot in the video at S10, and
warmth arrives from 5,000 km away. The emotional shape is claustrophobia releasing into scale.
That release is what people rewatch.

### Educational value
Delivers one durable mental model — **coastline is not access** — plus four named chokepoints
the viewer will now recognise in any future news story. That recognition is the subscribe
trigger described in Handbook 1.2.

### Retention strategy
| Risk point | Defence |
|---|---|
| 0–3s | Camera already moving at frame 1; no title card, no logo |
| 3s | The contradiction lands |
| 15s | First door resolves — proof the video pays out |
| 50% | The pattern is established; viewer is predicting |
| 68% | Reversal — the biggest camera move in the film |
| End | Visual rhyme with S3 closes the loop; hard cut, no outro to scroll away from |

### Replay value
The Gulf of Mexico reveal reframes everything before it, and the S13 visual rhyme sends the eye
back to S3. Second watches catch that the map went **dead grey** at 0:04 and only regains warmth
at 0:57 — a colour arc that isn't consciously readable the first time.

### Shareability
It settles arguments. "Why does Russia care so much about warm-water ports" is a question people
have actually had, and this is a 84-second answer with numbers on it. It is also
**politically un-loaded** — it makes no claim about anyone's intentions, only about where the
water is — which means it can be shared across a divided comment section without becoming a
fight. That is the difference between 200k views and 4M.
